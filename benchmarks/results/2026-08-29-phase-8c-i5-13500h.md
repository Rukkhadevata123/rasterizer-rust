# Phase 8C Benchmark Comparison

This comparison measures the committed Phase 8B renderer immediately before and after whole-pass mesh preparation on the same 13th Gen Intel Core i5-13500H with Rust 1.97.1 on Windows x64. Targeted runs used 5 warmup frames and 30 measured frames at 640x360. Negative change is faster.

| Scenario | Workers | Phase 8B mean ms | Phase 8C mean ms | Change | Hash preserved |
|:--|--:|--:|--:|--:|:--|
| city | 1 | 78.335 | 61.620 | -21.3% | yes |
| city | 12 | 80.797 | 19.477 | -75.9% | yes |
| many small triangles | 12 | 4.294 | 4.488 | +4.5% | yes |
| high transparency | 12 | 4.857 | 4.944 | +1.8% | yes |

The city result comes from preparation rather than a rasterization change. With 12 workers, shadow preparation changed from 34.161 to 5.965 ms and main preparation from 38.524 to 7.003 ms. The final standard 3-warmup/10-frame matrix independently changed city from the Phase 8B result of 64.193 ms to 20.273 ms with 12 workers, with the same `367cbeda8080c47f` output hash.

Small-scene preparation differences were below 0.1 ms and changed direction across repeated runs. Pure transparent queues therefore retain sequential preparation: their triangles are already globally sorted, have no per-mesh temporary collection, and are too light for another Rayon traversal. All representative matrix hashes remain identical to Phases 8A and 8B and match between one and 12 workers.
