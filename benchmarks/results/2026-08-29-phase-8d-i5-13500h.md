# Phase 8D Work-Partition Comparison

This comparison measures experimental 8-, 16-, and 32-row horizontal bands plus safe 16×16 and 32×16 2D tiles on the same 13th Gen Intel Core i5-13500H with Rust 1.97.1 on Windows x64. Every run used 5 warmup frames and 30 measured frames at 640×360 with one or 12 workers. Negative change is faster than the original 16-row bands.

## Twelve-worker results

The raster columns report combined opaque and transparent main-pass rasterization time. Shadow rasterization is measured separately by the benchmark and remains included in complete-frame time.

| Scenario | 8-row raster ms | 16-row raster ms | Raster change | 8-row frame ms | 16-row frame ms | Frame change |
|:--|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|
| Large triangle | 1.648 | 1.896 | -13.1% | 3.654 | 3.949 | -7.5% |
| 400 small triangles | 1.637 | 2.129 | -23.1% | 4.021 | 4.823 | -16.6% |
| Default car | 5.700 | 7.801 | -26.9% | 11.276 | 13.608 | -17.1% |
| Default car, 2× SSAA | 22.488 | 25.251 | -10.9% | 28.496 | 31.329 | -9.0% |
| City | 3.162 | 3.651 | -13.4% | 20.220 | 20.762 | -2.6% |
| High transparency | 2.431 | 3.133 | -22.4% | 4.930 | 5.828 | -15.4% |

At one worker, 8-row rasterization was between 3.2% faster and 3.1% slower than 16-row bands. This is small compared with its consistent 12-worker load-balance gain. The 32-row candidate lost badly on uneven shadow workloads, reaching 59.7% slower rasterization for the default car and 67.8% slower for the city at 12 workers.

## Tile decision

The experimental tile renderer divided each framebuffer row into disjoint mutable slices, grouped those slices by tile, and processed tiles in parallel. This preserved exclusive writes and transparent-triangle order without locks, atomics, or `unsafe`.

The 16×16 tile candidate beat 8-row bands on the default car complete frame (10.350 versus 11.276 ms) and its 2× SSAA variant (25.529 versus 28.496 ms). It was slower on the large triangle (3.831 versus 3.654 ms), 400 small triangles (4.569 versus 4.021 ms), and city (21.001 versus 20.220 ms), while high transparency was effectively tied. The 32×16 candidate followed the same pattern without a consistent advantage.

Tiles improved balance for the concentrated shadow-heavy car geometry, but their wider bin fan-out, large-triangle duplication, and per-draw tile-row assembly outweighed that benefit elsewhere. Since neither tile size won consistently across the matrix, Phase 8D retains only 8-row horizontal bands. The experimental selector and both tile backends were removed.

Every candidate produced the same per-scene output hash at one and 12 workers, including the globally sorted transparent scene.
