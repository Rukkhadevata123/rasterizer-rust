# Phase 8B Benchmark Comparison

This comparison uses the Phase 8A baseline and the completed Phase 8B working tree on the same 13th Gen Intel Core i5-13500H with Rust 1.97.1 on Windows x64. Both runs used 3 warmup frames, 10 measured frames, 640x360 output, and one versus 12 physical cores. Negative change is faster.

| Scenario | Phase 8A mean ms | Phase 8B mean ms | Change | Hash preserved |
|:--|--:|--:|--:|:--|
| large-triangle-threads-1 | 21.272 | 21.319 | +0.2% | yes |
| large-triangle-threads-12 | 3.303 | 3.235 | -2.1% | yes |
| many-small-triangles-threads-1 | 22.060 | 21.938 | -0.6% | yes |
| many-small-triangles-threads-12 | 3.842 | 3.946 | +2.7% | yes |
| default-car-threads-1 | 44.197 | 44.269 | +0.2% | yes |
| default-car-threads-12 | 12.259 | 12.366 | +0.9% | yes |
| default-car-2x-ssaa-threads-1 | 119.487 | 120.816 | +1.1% | yes |

| city-threads-1 | 77.709 | 74.517 | -4.1% | yes |

| high-transparency-threads-1 | 26.993 | 26.858 | -0.5% | yes |

The most direct preparation improvements are visible in the high-transparency main preparation stage: 1.274 to 0.641 ms with one worker and 1.800 to 0.471 ms with 12 workers. City shadow preparation improved from 25.307 to 21.730 ms with one worker. Results within a few percent should be treated as run-to-run noise; the stable hashes establish that the optimization did not alter output.
